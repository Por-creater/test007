K-Means Clustering

K-Means alternates two simple steps until nothing changes: 1) assign every point to its nearest centroid, and 2) update each centroid to the mean position of the points now assigned to it. It has no labels to learn from — it just discovers structure in unlabeled data.

"Inertia" is the sum of squared distances from every point to its centroid — it can only go down (or stay flat) as K-Means iterates, which is why the algorithm is guaranteed to converge, though not necessarily to the global optimum (try "New random points" a few times and watch centroids land differently).

Why initialization is a parameter too

Random init just picks k existing points at random as starting centroids — fast, but two centroids can land close together and split a cluster in half instead of covering the space. K-Means++ instead picks the first centroid randomly, then keeps picking new centroids weighted toward points that are far from any centroid chosen so far, spreading the starting positions out. It usually converges in fewer iterations to a lower final inertia.

k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN)

kNN has no training phase — it just remembers every labeled point. To classify a new point, it finds the k closest labeled points by distance and lets them vote by majority. Small k follows local noise; large k smooths the boundary but can blur real structure.

Choice of distance metric

Euclidean (straight-line) distance is the default. Manhattan distance sums the axis-aligned differences — it favors neighbors that are close on every feature rather than very close on one and far on another. Chebyshev distance only looks at the single largest axis difference. Switching metrics can change which points count as "nearest," and therefore the prediction, even with k and the data held fixed. Turning on distance-weighted voting lets closer neighbors outvote farther ones instead of every neighbor counting equally.

Distance-based, not model-based

Because kNN stores the whole dataset and computes distances at prediction time, it can represent arbitrarily complex boundaries — but prediction gets slower as the dataset grows, and it's sensitive to the scale of each feature (always normalize features before using kNN in practice).
